Chapter 5: Extra: Electronics and Control Basics

Gerald Lê Draft

01. Overview

Every robot, from a classroom rover to a six-axis industrial arm, has to solve the same basic problem: a program wants a motor to move a precise amount, but motors on their own only know how to spin. This chapter builds up the pieces that solve that problem, one article at a time.

If motors are a robot’s muscles, then PWM is the throttle, encoders are its sense of where its limbs are, and PID control is the reflex that connects the two. The examples use an Arduino because it is cheap, common and easy to experiment with, but the same ideas run inside industrial robot controllers.

02. What you will learn

  • Arduino: pins, the setup/loop structure, and how to wire motors and sensors safely.
  • PWM: how a digital pin controls motor speed and servo angle.
  • Encoders: how a robot measures how far and how fast its motors have turned.
  • PID control: how a robot uses that measurement to hit a target smoothly and accurately.

03. Articles in this chapter

  1. 5.1 Arduino for Robotics: The Arduino basics a robot programmer needs: pin types, the setup and loop structure, driving servos and DC motors, reading sensors and debugging.
  2. 5.2 Pulse Width Modulation (PWM): How PWM lets a digital pin control motor speed, servo angle and LED brightness, and the practical details that matter on a real robot.
  3. 5.3 Encoders and Shaft Encoders: How encoders let a robot measure how far and how fast its motors have turned: incremental vs. absolute, quadrature, the math, and common pitfalls.
  4. 5.4 PID Control: How a PID controller uses sensor feedback to move a robot smoothly and precisely: the three terms, a code example, tuning, and common pitfalls.
