Chapter 7: Machine Vision

Gerald Lê

01. Overview

In 2.8 Vision-Guided Picking, we examined how industrial robots use vision to locate workpieces that do not arrive in fixed positions. That lesson focused on the manipulator side: mounting cameras eye-in-hand or eye-to-hand, performing hand-eye calibration, and shifting taught points using vision results. Chapter 7 steps back to examine the vision system itself, covering the entire pipeline from physical photons to robot motion.

Machine vision begins in the physical environment. Industrial CMOS sensors with global shutters freeze rapid part motion on moving conveyors without rolling shutter distortion. Lenses must be matched to working distance, field of view, and required depth of field. Tailored illumination geometries—including backlighting, darkfield, glancing, diffuse dome, coaxial, and strobed lighting—create sharp optical contrast before any image processing algorithm runs. From there, 2D processing pipelines convert color spaces, segment parts via Otsu and adaptive thresholding, compute contour moments and centroids, and identify features with Canny edge detection and template matching.

For unstructured environments such as bin picking, the chapter introduces direct 3D sensing, stereo disparity geometry, and point cloud processing with PCL to segment supporting surfaces and extract object clusters. It concludes with automated inspection, dimensional gauging with telecentric optics, barcode quality verification under ISO standards, and a hands-on lab building an end-to-end ROS 2 camera pipeline in Python with cv_bridge.

02. Place in the course

Chapter 7 follows the Chapter 6 capstone projects and builds directly on Lesson 2.8 Vision-Guided Picking as well as the ROS 2 programming concepts established in Chapter 3. While Chapter 2 treated the vision sensor primarily as an external component providing coordinate offsets, this chapter equips you to specify optics, design lighting, process images, filter 3D point clouds, and integrate real-time camera streams into industrial robot cells.

03. Articles in this chapter

  1. 7.1 Industrial Cameras and Optics: Industrial camera sensors and optics: CMOS versus CCD, global and rolling shutters, pixel size and Nyquist resolution, focal length and f-number, telecentric lenses, and GenICam.
  2. 7.2 Lighting for Machine Vision: Illumination for machine vision: backlighting, ring, glancing, darkfield, diffuse dome, coaxial and in-line lighting, optical filters, cross-polarization, and LED strobing.
  3. 7.3 Image Processing Basics: Core 2D image processing: color space conversion, histograms and contrast equalization, global, Otsu and adaptive thresholding, contours, moments, blobs, Canny edge detection, and template matching.
  4. 7.4 3D Sensing: 3D perception for robotics: direct sensing versus 2D inference, stereo disparity geometry, point cloud filtering with PCL (PassThrough, VoxelGrid, outliers), RANSAC planes, Euclidean clustering, and bin picking.
  5. 7.5 Inspection and Measurement: Automated inspection and measurement: optical resolution and the Nyquist limit, telecentric gauging, lens distortion calibration, presence verification, and 1D/2D barcode quality.
  6. 7.6 Lab: A ROS 2 Camera Pipeline: Build a ROS 2 camera pipeline in Python: sensor_msgs/Image and CameraInfo, optical frames, image_transport, cv_bridge conversion, and real-time OpenCV edge processing; runs without a camera using cam2image test images.
