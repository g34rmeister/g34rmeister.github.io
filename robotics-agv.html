AGV Navigation & SLAM Architecture: From Odometry Fusion to Autonomous Corridor Traversal

Sep 28, 2026 Gerald Lê Jetson Orin Nano + STM32 6 min read Active Field Test

01. Overview & Operational Domain

The Autonomous Ground Vehicle (AGV) architecture is designed for autonomous intra-facility logistics, material transfer, and spatial map generation. Utilizing a ruggedized differential drive chassis paired with an onboard Jetson Orin Nano companion computer, the system executes real-time simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM).

The platform continuously monitors wheel odometry, spatial laser point clouds, and inertial heading vectors to maintain sub-centimeter positioning accuracy even across feature-sparse hallway environments.

Engineering Axiom

Robust real-world autonomy relies not on ideal sensors, but on gracefully handling sensor uncertainty through probabilistic state estimation, dynamic costmap inflation, and deterministic hardware watchdogs.

02. Navigation Stack & Sensor Fusion

Navigation is coordinated through the ROS 2 Nav2 framework operating over CycloneDDS. Sensor pipelines stream asynchronously through dedicated micro-services to keep computational latency predictable:

  • Global Path Planning (Smac & NavFn): A* graph search across pre-computed 2D occupancy grids with dynamic inflation barriers to guarantee clearance from static infrastructure.
  • Local Trajectory Generation (RPP): Regulated Pure Pursuit controller computing kinematically feasible velocity commands while enforcing dynamic deceleration upon detecting transient obstacles.
  • State Estimation (Dual EKF @ 100 Hz): Continuous probabilistic sensor fusion combining differential wheel quadrature encoders with 9-DOF BNO055 orientation quaternion updates.
  • Safety Watchdog (< 0.25 m Proximity): Dedicated hardware emergency-stop interrupt bypassing software stack to disengage motor gate drivers directly when laser scan thresholds drop below safe limits.

03. Low-Level Firmware & Motor Actuation

At the base embedded tier, an STM32 ARM Cortex-M4 microcontroller executes dual closed-loop PID velocity controllers at 1 kHz. Hall effect quadrature encoders provide continuous shaft position feedback to the brushed DC drive motors.

  • STM32 Cortex-M4 Firmware (1 kHz Loop): Dual closed-loop PID velocity controllers providing sub-millisecond motor commutation and encoder pulse capture.
  • micro-ROS Transport (UART @ 921600 Baud): Deterministic serial transport bridging the Jetson Linux host and STM32 microcontroller with under 2.0 ms latency.
  • Power Distribution & Telemetry (24V LiFePO4): Onboard current sensing with automatic low-voltage safety cutoff and real-time battery fuel gauge streaming.

04. Empirical Benchmarking & Key Takeaways

Empirical testing across warehouse floor plans demonstrated average localization drift under 1.2% over 500-meter closed trajectories. Automatic recovery behaviors—including in-place rotation clearing and dynamic replanning—enabled the vehicle to reliably traverse high-traffic human corridors without manual intervention.
