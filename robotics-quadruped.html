Quadruped Locomotion Engine: Convex Model Predictive Control & Whole-Body Dynamics

Aug 28, 2026 Gerald Lê Python + PyBullet + qpOASES 6 min read Simulation Benchmark

01. Overview & Dynamic Locomotion

The Quadruped Dynamic Locomotion Engine is a high-speed simulation and control framework designed to achieve stable, agile trotting and bound locomotion across uneven, compliant terrains. Powered by convex Model Predictive Control (MPC) paired with whole-body inverse dynamics, the platform recalculates optimal foot contact forces at 50 Hz.

Each of the 4 legs features 3 actuated degrees of freedom (hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch), driven by quasi-direct drive (QDD) actuators offering low mechanical impedance and backdrivability.

Engineering Axiom

Dynamic quadrupedal stability is not achieved by rigid joint stiffness, but by treating the robot as a simplified rigid body whose centroidal momentum is governed by optimal ground contact forces.

02. Convex MPC & Whole-Body Control

The control architecture separates high-level ground reaction force optimization from joint-level trajectory tracking:

  • Convex Model Predictive Control: Formulating centroidal momentum dynamics into a quadratic program (QP) solved in under 2.5 ms using qpOASES over a 10-step horizon.
  • Swing Leg Kinematics: Cubic Bezier spline trajectory generation ensuring smooth foot lift-off and zero ground impact velocity during touchdown.
  • Whole-Body Control (WBC): Hierarchical null-space projection prioritizing contact force limits, base posture, and foot placement.
  • Terrain Adaptation: Heuristic Raibert footstep placement adjusted for linear body velocity and external lateral disturbances.

03. Physics Simulation in PyBullet

Prior to physical actuation, the complete URDF model was simulated in PyBullet with custom contact constraint solvers. Synthetic obstacles, including ramps, steps, and random lateral impulse forces, validated the controller's recovery robustness.

04. Benchmarking & Future Hardware Deployment

In simulation benchmarks, the quadruped achieved forward trot velocities up to 1.8 m/s with successful recovery from unexpected lateral impulses up to 45 N. Ongoing work focuses on porting the trajectory solver to real-time embedded Linux on an STM32MP1 processor.
