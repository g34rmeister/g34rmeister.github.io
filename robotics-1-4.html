1.4 How to Use This Course

Gerald Lê Draft

01. Reading order

The chapters build on each other, so the simplest path is to read them in order:

  1. Chapter 1: Introduction gives the vocabulary.
  2. Chapter 2: Electronics and Control Basics explains how a program makes a motor move precisely. Read Arduino, then PWM, then encoders, then PID; each one uses the previous.
  3. Chapter 3: Programming Industrial Robots moves from hobby hardware to real industrial arms and how they are programmed in practice.

If you only care about industrial robots, you can start at Chapter 3. Come back to Chapter 2 when a term like “encoder” or “control loop” comes up.

02. How articles are laid out

Every article opens with a contents box and is split into short numbered sections. Callout boxes flag rules of thumb and common mistakes. Articles end with references, and the arrows at the bottom of each page step to the previous and next article.

03. Topics planned next

These topics are on the list for future articles:

  • Robot types and applications: articulated arms, collaborative robots and other common industrial designs, and what each is used for.
  • Safety: how industrial and collaborative robots keep people out of harm’s way.
  • Sensors: ultrasonic sensors, color sensors, inertial measurement units (IMUs) and cameras.
  • Estimation: odometry and Kalman filters.
  • Program structure: state machines and motion interpolation.
