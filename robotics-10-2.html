      10.2 Costmaps: How the Robot Sees Its Surroundings

      Gerald Lê
      Chapter 10, lesson 2 of 5 ← Previous: 10.1 Where Is the Robot? Frames, Odometry and Localization Next: 10.3 Planners, Controllers and Recoveries →

      01. One shared picture of the world

      The environmental representation is the way the robot perceives its environment.[1] It is also where different algorithms and data sources combine their information into a single space, which the controllers, planners and recoveries then use to compute their tasks.[1]

      02. The costmap

      In Nav2 the environmental representation is a costmap: a regular 2D grid of cells, each holding a cost from unknown, free, occupied or inflated.[1] The costmap is searched to compute a global plan, or sampled to compute local control efforts.[1]

      03. Layers and inflation

      A costmap is built from layers. Costmap layers are plugins that buffer information into the costmap, including information from lidar, radar, sonar and depth images.[1] Layers can also be written to detect and track obstacles using camera or depth sensors, or to change the underlying costmap by some rule or heuristic.[1]

      The inflation layer is one of those rule-based layers. It places a decaying cost around obstacles, which makes it more costly to travel near a collision.[2] Its inflation_radius parameter sets the radius to inflate around lethal obstacles.[2]

      04. Two costmaps and one robot shape

      Nav2 keeps a global and a local view. The planner has access to a global environmental representation, and the controller to a local one.[3] The behavior server subscribes to the local costmap instead of building its own, because costmaps are computationally expensive to duplicate.[3] 10.3 covers those three servers.

      The costmap also needs the robot’s shape. The footprint is set either as a circle of radius robot_radius or, for a non-circular robot, as a list of points called footprint that describes a polygon.[3] The footprint can change while the robot runs, for example when it picks up a pallet, and the planners and controllers then use the new polygon automatically.[3]

      05. Costmap filters: rules painted on the map

      A filter mask is an annotated map that marks areas where something different should happen.[1] Costmap filters are costmap plugins that read the mask and change the robot’s behavior depending on where it is.[1] Nav2’s documentation gives three examples: keep-out zones the robot will never enter, speed restriction areas, and preferred lanes for robots moving in industrial environments and warehouses.[1]

      06. Other representations

      Costmaps are not the only option. Nav2 lists gradient maps, 3D costmaps (which then also need 3D planning and collision checking) and mesh maps as other forms.[1]

      07. Check your understanding

      • What four kinds of cost can a costmap cell hold?
        Show answer

        Each cell holds a cost that is unknown, free, occupied or inflated. (Section 02)

      • What does the inflation layer do, and which parameter sets how far it reaches?
        Show answer

        The inflation layer places a decaying cost around obstacles, which makes it more costly to travel near a collision. Its inflation_radius parameter sets how far it reaches. (Section 03)

      • Name two things a costmap filter can make a robot do in a marked area.
        Show answer

        It can keep a robot out of an area it will never enter (a keep-out zone), or restrict its speed in a marked area. (Section 05)

      Try it
      1. A warehouse has a pedestrian walkway the robots must not use and a loading dock where they must slow down. Which Nav2 feature handles both, and what would you draw on the filter mask for each?
      2. A robot picks up a pallet that is wider than the robot. What must change in the costmap configuration while it carries the pallet, and which Nav2 parts use the change?
      3. On squared paper, draw a 10 by 6 costmap with one obstacle. Mark the occupied cells, then the cells you would expect the inflation layer to raise.
