Chapter 2: Robot Programming

Gerald Lê Draft

01. Overview

Industrial robots are programmed very differently from a hobby rover. Instead of writing every line yourself, you work through the tools each robot maker provides: a handheld teach pendant, a graphical interface, a simulator on a PC, and a vendor-specific programming language. This chapter walks through those tools and ends with ROS, which works across vendors.

02. What you will learn

  • Types of programming: online and offline programming, and when to use each.
  • Environments: ABB RobotStudio and Universal Robots PolyScope.
  • Languages: URScript, RAPID and RRL.
  • ROS: a vendor-agnostic layer on top of all of them.

03. Articles in this chapter

  1. 2.1 Types of Programming: The two ways industrial robots are programmed: offline in simulation software, and online with a teach pendant or by hand guiding.
  2. 2.2 Common Programming Environments: Two widely used robot programming environments: ABB RobotStudio for offline programming and simulation, and Universal Robots PolyScope for cobots.
  3. 2.3 Common Programming Languages: Industrial robot languages: Universal Robots' URScript, ABB's RAPID, and RRL.
  4. 2.4 Intro to ROS (Vendor-Agnostic): Why ROS exists: a common, vendor-agnostic layer for programming robots from different makers.
