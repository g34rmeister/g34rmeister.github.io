Chapter 8: Industrial Networks

Gerald Lê

01. Overview

1.7 The Industrial Robot Cell introduced the handshake signals between a robot and the cell PLC, and 2.5 End-of-Arm Tooling and I/O the digital I/O on the controller and tool flange. Chapter 8 moves those signals from separate wires onto networks.

The chapter begins with electrical wiring rules and network architecture. Sinking (NPN) and sourcing (PNP) DC circuits govern how field devices share power and common return paths. As machine complexity grew, serial fieldbuses replaced bundles of point-to-point wires, and industrial Ethernet expanded this capability by running multiple communication protocols across shared physical media in line, star, and ring configurations. It then covers four industrial Ethernet protocols: EtherNet/IP with CIP implicit UDP and explicit TCP messaging, PROFINET with real-time (RT) channels and GSDML device description files, EtherCAT processing frames on the fly, and Modbus TCP with its four register tables and 7-byte MBAP header.

From protocol foundations, the chapter moves into hands-on cell integration and plant-level connectivity. We map controller handshakes into Modbus TCP registers, packing multi-bit signals with URScript and communicating from Python using pymodbus. We then examine functional safety networks—including PROFIsafe, FailSafe over EtherCAT (FSoE), and CIP Safety—exploring how the black channel principle transports safety commands over standard networks. Finally, we examine plant-floor integration via IO-Link, OPC UA with the VDMA 40010 Robotics companion specification, MQTT telemetry, and industrial security under ISA/IEC 62443, ending with a hands-on Wireshark diagnostic capture.

02. Place in the course

Chapter 8 follows Chapter 7 and builds on 1.7 (the cell PLC and handshakes), 2.5 (controller and tool I/O) and 5.4 (cell safety). The two labs, 8.3 and 8.6, need only Python and Wireshark on your own computer.

03. Articles in this chapter

  1. 8.1 From Wires to Networks: From discrete wiring to industrial networks: sinking and sourcing (NPN/PNP), fieldbus history, line, star and ring topologies, cyclic versus acyclic data, and Device Level Ring recovery.
  2. 8.2 Industrial Ethernet Protocols: Industrial Ethernet protocols: EtherNet/IP CIP implicit and explicit messaging, PROFINET RT and IRT with GSDML, EtherCAT on-the-fly frame processing with ESI, and Modbus TCP.
  3. 8.3 Lab: Connecting a Robot to a PLC: Lab: the UR Modbus TCP server and its general-purpose registers, packing handshake bits in URScript, and writing and reading registers from Python with pymodbus (stand-in server, no hardware).
  4. 8.4 Safety over the Network: Functional safety over industrial networks: the black channel principle, PROFIsafe, FailSafe over EtherCAT (FSoE), CIP Safety, and stop category classifications.
  5. 8.5 From the Plant Floor to IT: Integrating plant-floor devices with enterprise IT: IO-Link sensor architecture, OPC UA information modeling and the VDMA 40010 robotics specification, MQTT telemetry, and ros2_control.
  6. 8.6 Network Design, Security and Diagnostics: Network design, security and diagnostics: ISA/IEC 62443, zones and the DMZ, VLANs, Time-Sensitive Networking, and a Wireshark lab that decodes the 8.3 Modbus traffic.

04. Chapter quiz

After completing the lessons, take the Chapter 8 quiz to test your understanding of sinking and sourcing wiring, industrial Ethernet protocols, Modbus TCP register maps, network safety, OPC UA and MQTT integration, and network security diagnostics.
