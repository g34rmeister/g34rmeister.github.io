      Chapter 11: Mobile Robots and Navigation

      Gerald Lê

      01. Overview

      The chapters before this one are mostly about robot arms that stay in one place. Chapter 11 is about robots that drive: the autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) that 1.3 Types of Robots set apart from AGVs. It follows Nav2, the open-source ROS 2 navigation project, and every lesson is built on Nav2’s own documentation.

      The four lessons follow a navigation request from the bottom up: how the robot knows where it is, how it represents obstacles, how it plans and follows a path, and how a behavior tree ties those pieces together. The chapter has no lab yet, because robOSStics only publishes lab code that has been run.

      02. Place in the course

      Chapter 11 builds on 3.5 Case Study: Contributing to Nav2 (what Nav2 is and how to contribute to it), Chapter 4 (ROS 2 nodes, topics and launch files) and three lessons from Chapter 6: 6.5 Sensors for Robots, 6.6 Odometry and 6.7 State Machines. It does not cover the safety of mobile robots; safety content in this course is in 5.4 Making a Robot Cell Safe and 8.4 Safety over the Network.

      03. Articles in this chapter

      1. 11.1 Where Is the Robot? Frames, Odometry and Localization: The map, odom and base_link frames from REP 105, why odometry drifts, and how localization or SLAM corrects it.
      2. 11.2 Costmaps: How the Robot Sees Its Surroundings: Costmap cells, layers and inflation, the robot footprint, and costmap filters such as keep-out zones and speed limits.
      3. 11.3 Planners, Controllers and Recoveries: The Nav2 servers that plan a path, smooth it, follow it and recover when the robot gets stuck, plus waypoint following.
      4. 11.4 Behavior Trees, Actions and Lifecycle Nodes: How a navigation request travels through the NavigateToPose action and the behavior tree, and how lifecycle nodes start and stop in order.

      04. Chapter quiz

      After completing the lessons, take the Chapter 11 quiz to check what you remember about frames, costmaps, the navigation servers and behavior trees.
