robOSStics

robOSStics is a web-based course that teaches students how to program robots, with a focus on industrial robotics. It starts with what robots are and where they are used, shows how industrial robots are programmed in practice (teach pendants, offline simulation, and languages like URScript and RAPID), introduces ROS, and closes with the ethics of building robots.

Course Contents

Chapters

Four chapters that follow the course roadmap, an optional extra chapter on electronics and control that the earlier lessons point into, a chapter of capstone projects, and added chapters on machine vision, industrial networks, and maintenance and troubleshooting. They are meant to be read in order. Each chapter page gives an overview, and each article is a short, self-contained lesson.

About the Project

robOSStics is being built by Gerald Lê as an undergraduate honors independent research project (CSC 3991) in the Department of Computer Science at Louisiana State University in Spring 2026, advised by Felipe Fronchetti. It is written for students who can already program a little but have not worked with robots, and it follows a roadmap from the basics of robotics through industrial robot programming, ROS and ethics, then ends with three capstone projects that put the pieces together.
