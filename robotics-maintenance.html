Chapter 9: Maintenance and Troubleshooting

Gerald Lê

01. Overview

1.7 The Industrial Robot Cell introduced the layout, safeguarding, and controller interfaces of automated manufacturing cells, and 4.4 Making a Robot Cell Safe established hazardous energy control and stop categories. Chapter 9 addresses what technicians and engineers do to keep industrial robot cells operating reliably and how to diagnose and resolve faults when equipment stops.

The chapter opens with maintenance strategies and structured troubleshooting procedures. We compare scheduled preventive maintenance, condition-based predictive maintenance, and run-to-failure reactive maintenance, evaluating their impact on unplanned downtime and part quality. We examine the five-step hazardous energy control sequence under OSHA standard 29 CFR 1910.147 (lockout/tagout), controller event logs, error code ranges, and high-speed flight records to isolate root causes without masking recurring faults.

Next, the chapter investigates physical failure modes across mechanical, electrical, and sensor subsystems. Building on the gearbox mechanisms and repeatability concepts from 1.6 Robot Anatomy and Coordinate Frames and the tooling interfaces from 2.5 End-of-Arm Tooling and I/O, we diagnose dress pack routing, bend radii, seal degradation, and joint brake engagement failures. We then address electrical faults, using multimeters to test voltage and continuity, checking internal and external 24 V power supply limits, and diagnosing discrete sensors configured for sinking and sourcing circuits introduced in 8.1 From Wires to Networks.

Finally, we cover axis recalibration, TCP verification, and controller disaster recovery before concluding with software diagnostics. Technicians learn to update axis revolution counters at physical synchronization marks, calibrate tool centers, manage joint replacement mismatches, and automate system backups using flash storage images and magic files. In the hands-on lab, students connect to the Universal Robots Dashboard Server over a TCP/IP socket on port 29999 from Python, querying robot modes, monitoring safety states, and executing controlled recovery sequences following protective stops.

02. Place in the course

Chapter 9 follows Chapter 8 and builds on 1.6 Robot Anatomy and Coordinate Frames (gearboxes and repeatability), 1.7 The Industrial Robot Cell (cell layout and safety fences), 2.5 End-of-Arm Tooling and I/O (tooling and sensor signals), 4.4 Making a Robot Cell Safe (hazardous energy control and stop categories), 8.1 From Wires to Networks (sinking and sourcing circuits), and 8.3 Lab: Connecting a Robot to a PLC (controller register communication). The lab in 9.6 requires only Python on your computer with no specialized hardware.

03. Articles in this chapter

  1. 9.1 Preventive Maintenance: Preventive, predictive and reactive maintenance strategies: inspection intervals, cleaning and ESD protection, fastener torque checks, and maintenance records.
  2. 9.2 A Troubleshooting Method: A systematic troubleshooting method: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 lockout/tagout, controller error logs, flight records, and avoiding masked faults.
  3. 9.3 Mechanical Problems: Mechanical fault diagnosis: cable dress packs and carriers, bend radii, seal inspection, collision recovery in backdrive mode, and joint brake engagement.
  4. 9.4 Electrical and I/O Faults: Electrical and I/O troubleshooting: multimeter voltage and continuity checks, discrete sensor wiring, 24 V power budgets, and fieldbus diagnostics.
  5. 9.5 Calibration, Backups and Recovery: Calibration, backups and recovery: axis revolution counter updates, TCP calibration, joint replacement alignment, and controller storage backups.
  6. 9.6 Lab: Check a Robot's State from Python: Lab: querying the Universal Robots Dashboard Server over TCP port 29999 with Python to check robot mode, safety status, and recover from protective stops.
